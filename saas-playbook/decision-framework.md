# Decision Framework

## Purpose

When a decision comes up — big or small — use this framework to determine the right answer. No debate. No committee meetings. Run it through the filter and act.

## The Three Lenses

Every business decision falls into one of three categories. Use the lens specific to that category.

### Lens 1: Build/Ship Decisions

**When:** Deciding what product feature to build, what to cut, what to ship next.

**Rules:**
1. Build only after 10+ paying customers or beta testers have explicitly asked for it
2. Score each request: value of requester (1-10), frequency (1-10), dev time (hours)
3. Build the highest (value x frequency) / dev_time ratio first
4. If you can do it manually, don't build it yet
5. The feature should make customers more or less likely to be "livid" if we disappeared?

**Kill criteria:** If a feature isn't requested 3+ times by different customers in a month, it's on hold.

### Lens 2: Growth/Marketing Decisions

**When:** Choosing how to acquire customers, what channels to test, what content to create.

**Rules:**
1. Before 1M ARR: only 1-2 acquisition channels at a time (manual outreach, cold email, LinkedIn)
2. No paid ads until PMF is confirmed
3. No SEO until organic growth is happening
4. No influencer partnerships until PMF
5. Post daily on LinkedIn/Twitter — not for reach, to find content-market fit
6. Every channel test must answer: cost per customer, quality of customer, retention rate

**Kill criteria:** Any channel with CAC that isn't recoverable within 90 days gets cut.

### Lens 3: Operations/Hiring Decisions

**When:** Deciding to hire, spend, automate, or restructure.

**Rules:**
1. **Before 1M ARR:** Only co-founders. No exceptions.
2. **1M-5M ARR:** Hire for gaps, not for growth. One role at a time.
3. **5M-10M ARR:** Still lean. 10 employees max.
4. Automate only after 6 months of doing it manually
5. Every hire must pass: "Does this make the business more replaceable?"
6. Every expense must pass: "Could we survive without this for 6 months?"

**Kill criteria:** Any role that isn't directly tied to revenue or customer retention gets eliminated first.

## The Four-Question Filter

No matter what kind of decision, ask these four questions:

| Question | What it tests | Kill the idea if |
|----------|---------------|------------------|
| 1. Does a paying customer need this? | Product-market fit | Answer is "maybe" or "hypothetically" |
| 2. Does this make us more replaceable? | E-Myth principle | Answer is "no, it makes me more essential" |
| 3. Can we afford this without outside money? | Bootstrap discipline | Answer is "we'd need to raise" |
| 4. Would this increase our exit valuation? | Exit focus | Answer is "no" or "irrelevant" |

**If 2 or more answers are "no" — the decision is killed.**

## Priority Order

When everything is urgent (and it always is), decide based on this order:

1. **Customer conversations** — always #1 priority
2. **Revenue-generating activities** — shipping, selling, onboarding
3. **System documentation** — SOPs, playbooks, processes
4. **Growth experiments** — testing channels, content creation
5. **Nice-to-haves** — polish, optimization, "efficiency"

## Time Allocation Guideline

How should the CEO spend their time?

| Company Stage | Customer Convos | Building/Product | Systems/Docs | Growth/Marketing | Admin |
|---------------|-----------------|------------------|--------------|------------------|-------|
| 0-100K | 40% | 20% | 10% | 20% | 10% |
| 100K-1M | 25% | 20% | 20% | 20% | 15% |
| 1M-5M | 15% | 15% | 20% | 25% | 25% |
| 5M-10M | 10% | 10% | 20% | 30% | 30% |

**Warning:** If CEO spending <20% time on customer conversations after 1M ARR, something is wrong.

## Red Flags

These signals mean you've gone off-track. If you see any, stop and reset:

- [ ] CEO spent last week building without talking to a customer
- [ ] Hiring happened before 1M ARR
- [ ] Burn rate exceeded monthly revenue
- [ ] Feature built without paying beta testers requesting it
- [ ] Marketing spend with no proven channel yet
- [ ] CEO feels irreplaceable in daily operations
- [ ] Team grew but revenue didn't grow proportionally
- [ ] "Competitor launched X" is the only reason you're doing something

## Escalation

Only two decisions require consensus (not debate):
1. **Product pivot** — moving to a different problem/market
2. **Exit readiness** — deciding when to start selling

Everything else: CEO decides. Act fast. Correct course later.
