# Operational Cadence

## Purpose

Rhythms that keep the business running on autopilot. If you follow these, the business runs itself. That's the E-Myth goal: systems over heroics.

## Weekly Rhythm

### Monday Morning — Plan (30 min)

Review last week's numbers. Set this week's ONE priority.

Questions:
- What did we ship last week?
- How many customer conversations did we have?
- What's the ONE thing that moves the needle most this week?

### Tuesday-Thursday — Execution

**Daily customer conversation:** At least 1 per day. Minimum.
**Daily LinkedIn posting:** 1 post per day. Founder-led. Real, not polished.
**Work in public:** Document what you're building, learning, failing at.

### Friday Afternoon — Review (30 min)

Same as the CEO Weekly Review:
- What did we ship?
- How many customer conversations?
- What did we learn?
- What did we delete/disable/shut down?
- Are our systems better or worse than last week?
- What's the one thing that would move the needle most next week?

## Monthly Rhythm

### First Monday — Metrics Review (1 hour)

Review:
- ARR / MRR trend
- New customers acquired
- Churn rate (and talk to every churn customer)
- CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) by channel
- LTV (if you can estimate it)
- Cash in bank (runway in months)
- Headcount vs revenue per employee

### Second Week — Product Review (1 hour)

- Review feature request spreadsheet (value x frequency / dev time)
- What are we building next? Why?
- What are we cutting? Why?
- Talk to 5 beta testers / power users
- Look for "magic moments" — where are customers thrilled?

### Third Week — Growth Review (1 hour)

- Which acquisition channel is performing best?
- Double down on winners. Kill losers.
- Test 1 new channel per month
- Review content performance (not vanity metrics — engagement quality)

### Fourth Week — Systems Review (1 hour)

- What process needs an SOP?
- What manual task can we document this month?
- Are we still doing things that don't scale? (That's good if pre-PMF)
- Is the CEO still the bottleneck on anything?

## Quarterly Rhythm

### Quarterly Business Review (QBR) — Full Day (or half-day)

#### Part 1: Strategy Check
- Are we still solving the same problem for the same customer?
- Has the market changed?
- Do we need to pivot or adjust positioning?

#### Part 2: Financial Review
- Revenue vs target
- Burn rate vs revenue
- Profitability trajectory
- Runway calculation

#### Part 3: Product Health
- NPS score
- Retention curves (cohort analysis)
- Feature adoption rates
- Customer "lividity" score

#### Part 4: Systems Health
- How many SOPs written vs needed?
- CEO time allocation review
- Hiring plan for next quarter
- Any key-person dependencies?

#### Part 5: Exit Readiness Assessment
- If we put the business on the market today, what would buyers say?
- Revenue growth rate (3x ARR multiple minimum)
- Profit margins (15%+ for bootstrap)
- Customer concentration (no single customer >20% of revenue)
- Systems maturity (can someone run this for 30 days without us?)
- Team quality and depth

## Annual Rhythm

### Annual Planning (2 days)

1. **Retrospective** — What worked? What didn't? What surprised us?
2. **Set annual targets** — Revenue, headcount, features, channels
3. **Update mission** — Does it still apply?
4. **Exit timeline review** — Are we on track for 36-60 month exit?
5. **Personal review** — How is the CEO doing? Burned out? Too comfortable?

## Key Performance Indicators

Track these numbers religiously. Update weekly.

### Revenue Metrics
- MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue)
- ARR (Annual Run Rate)
- Growth rate month-over-month
- Churn rate %
- Net Revenue Retention %

### Customer Metrics
- New customers per month
- Active users / total users
- NPS score
- Support tickets per 100 users
- Customer lifetime value (estimate)

### Operational Metrics
- Revenue per employee
- CEO time on customer conversations
- Number of SOPs written
- Time to ship (feature request to production)
- Burn rate / cash runway

## Meeting Rules

To keep things lean (no B2B SaaS startup bloat):

1. **No standups** — Slack/async updates are fine
2. **No more than 2 meetings/week** — one weekly review, one quarterly review
3. **No agendas needed** — if you call a meeting, you know what it's about
4. **No attendees needed who can read a summary** — if it doesn't require discussion, send an email
5. **Time-box everything** — 30 min weekly, 1 hour monthly, half-day quarterly

## Automation Rules

When to automate vs stay manual:

| Rule | Action |
|------|--------|
| Doing something 3+ times manually? | Document it (SOP) |
| SOP written and tested 3+ times? | Consider automation |
| Customer paying for this already? | Yes, automate |
| Pre-revenue or pre-PMF? | Stay manual |
| Automation saves <5 hrs/week? | Don't automate |
